It has a name

The changes nobody
explains beforehand.

Three things that show up in the first year after bariatric surgery, what they're actually called, and roughly how long each one takes to turn over.

Hair that sheds

Telogen effluvium

Rapid weight loss pushes a large share of follicles into their resting phase at once, so they shed together a few months later. It starts around month three — and it usually passes.

Biotin 6,000 mcg, marine collagen, silicon, zinc and vitamin E — nutrients that support hair health.

Recovery: months, not weeks

Nails that split and peel

Onychoschizia

Layered splitting at the tip. Rapid weight loss and shifting nutrient status are common triggers — and nails turn over faster than hair, so they often show change first.

Silicon, biotin and zinc — nutrients that support nail strength.

A fingernail fully replaces itself in about six months

Skin that feels tight and dry

Xerosis

Dry, tight, flaky skin. Rapid weight loss, reduced intake and fluid restrictions around meals are all common triggers.

Vitamin A 133% DV, vitamin E, zinc and vitamin C — the last two contribute to normal collagen formation.

Skin renews itself about every four weeks

Hair loss after metabolic and bariatric surgery affects roughly half of patients in published meta-analyses. It is typically self-limiting. Nutrient support is not a treatment for any of the conditions named above.

Why protein gets hard

Your stomach can't
break down protein
the way it used to.

After bariatric surgery, your pouch produces less acid and less pepsin — and pepsin is the enzyme that starts protein breakdown. That's why fibrous protein becomes the hardest thing on the plate. It's mechanical, not willpower.
